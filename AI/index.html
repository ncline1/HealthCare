Week 6: Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

Welcome to Week 6. As we transition from digital records to intelligent systems, we explore how Artificial Intelligence (AI) is moving from theoretical research to bedside application. This week, we focus on three core pillars transforming care delivery: Diagnostic Precision, Operational Efficiency, and Proactive Intervention.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the role of Deep Learning in Diagnostic Imaging (CAD).
  • Analyze how NLP automates Clinical Documentation.
  • Evaluate the impact of Predictive Analytics on patient outcomes.
  • Activity: Simulate a Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS).

1. Diagnostic Imaging & Computer Vision

Medical imaging (X-Ray, CT, MRI) generates massive datasets. AI, specifically Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), excels at pattern recognition, identifying anomalies like tumors, fractures, or bleeds often faster than the human eye.

Crucially, AI is not replacing radiologists but augmenting them. The concept of "Augmented Intelligence" suggests that the combination of human judgment and AI sensitivity yields the highest accuracy.

Key Concept: Sensitivity vs. Specificity. AI often has higher sensitivity (finds everything) but may have lower specificity (false positives), requiring human verification.

2. Automating Clinical Documentation

Physician burnout is a crisis, often driven by the "pajama time" spent on Electronic Health Records (EHR). Natural Language Processing (NLP) creates "Ambient Clinical Intelligence." These systems listen to the patient-doctor conversation, transcribe it, extract medical terms, and structure a SOAP note automatically.

3. Predictive Analytics & Early Intervention

Descriptive analytics tells us what happened. Predictive analytics tells us what will happen. By analyzing vitals, labs, and history in real-time, AI can flag deteriorating patients hours before a human notices clinical signs of sepsis or cardiac arrest.

Case Study

Early Disease Detection: Diabetic Retinopathy

The Challenge: Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of blindness. It requires specialized eye exams that are scarce in rural areas.

The AI Solution: Google Health developed a deep learning algorithm trained on 128,000 retinal images. The AI grades images on a 5-point scale for severity.

The Outcome: Deployment in India and Thailand allowed technicians to scan eyes, with the AI providing instant diagnostic grading. Patients with "referable" DR were flagged immediately, increasing screening rates by 40% and treating patients before vision loss occurred.
