The Infrastructure Divide

Before patients can benefit from digital health, they must be able to connect to it. The "digital divide" remains the primary barrier to healthcare equity. This chart compares critical infrastructure and utilization metrics across urban and rural demographics, highlighting a compounding disadvantage.

Algorithmic Bias in Risk Prediction

Machine learning models often use historical healthcare spending as a proxy for a patient's health needs. Because marginalized populations historically face barriers to care and spend less, algorithms falsely predict they are healthier than they actually are, systematically denying them necessary proactive care.

Actual Health Risk vs. Algorithm-Assigned Risk Score

●

Majority Demographic: The algorithm correctly aligns their high actual health risk with a high predicted risk score, ensuring they receive care management.

●

Marginalized Demographic: Despite having the same or worse actual health risk, the algorithm assigns a severely deflated risk score, resulting in undercare.

The Privacy Illusion

Consumers widely believe that all health data is protected. However, data generated by commercial wearables and wellness apps often falls completely outside standard medical privacy laws, creating a lucrative, unregulated shadow market for sensitive biometric data.
